With a modern look, Goodnotes aims to make it easier to access and use all of the tools you love, and the new ones you’ll discover. This guide will help you get familiar with the main interface.

## Library View

## Document View
From the Document View, you can get to the Library View via the Home icon in the top left corner.

### Document Tab Bar
Located at the top of the screen, the Document Tab Bar displays all of your open documents (by default). You can switch between documents and close them from here. 

### Nav Bar
The Nav Bar is located right below the Document Tab Bar and provides quick access to different sections of the app.
The Nav Bar consists of three sections:
- The left section offers access to the Sidebar (for managing pages), Search, Goodnotes AI, and View Modes (Edit or Read-only).
- The middle section houses the ‣.
- The right section contains the menus of Add Page, Share & Export, and More.

### Toolbar
Tools are organized into simple categories based on their function. Most tools have a dedicated secondary-level menu called the Active Tool Menu, making it easy to access any specific tool you might need.
The Toolbar has four main parts:
- Lasso Selection - Lets you select and work with objects in your document.
- Tools - Contains all the basic tools for writing, drawing, and adding content.
- The Accessories Menu - Provides access to other useful features:
> 
Some accessories may not be available on all platforms. For example, Time Keeper and Zoom Window aren’t currently available in Goodnotes for Android, Windows, and Web.
> 
You can customize the visibility and order of these tools from the Accessories menu > Toolbar Customization.
- Active Tool Menu - A helpful menu that appears when you select certain tools, showing you extra options and settings.
In the right-hand part of the Active Tool Menu is the contextual section, which lists the relevant properties of the selected sub-tool. For example, if you select the Pen Tool, you’ll see several color and thickness slots on the right that you can choose from for your strokes.
> 
- You can drag the Active Tool Menu to any of the four sides of the screen.
- If you’re using Apple Pencil Pro on a supported iPad, you can squeeze the Pencil to open the toolbar. Other stylus shortcuts (e.g., Samsung S Pen or Surface pen buttons) may be available depending on your device and platform.

#### Sticky vs. non-sticky Tools
Sticky tools remain active after use, allowing you to continue using the same tool without reselection. For example, when using the Pen tool, you can write continuously across multiple pages without having to reselect the tool. These tools are sticky: Lasso, Writing Tools (Pen, Eraser, Pencil, Highlighter, Tape), and Laser Pointer.
Non-sticky tools automatically revert to the Lasso tool after a single use, which is ideal for insertion tasks that typically require immediate editing after placement. This also allows you to quickly select any other object on the canvas without switching the tools. The non-sticky tools are: Images, Elements, Shapes, Sticky Notes, and Text (enters Typing state after insertion so you can type immediately).

#### Move around your document

## Record audio

Having trouble keeping up with meetings or lectures? Record audio to capture what you miss and enhance your notes. You can also replay the recording to see your notes come to life as they were written!
Goodnotes’ Audio capabilities include:
- Audio Recording
- Audio Transcription
- Audio Summary

### Audio Recording

#### Start / Stop a recording
1. Choose the Overflow menu > Record & Summarize.
> 
For a ‣, you can simply tap the Mic icon on the toolbar.
1. A floating control appears showing the recording is in progress. Notes taken in the document will be recorded with the audio. On all platforms, Goodnotes shows a microphone/recording status indicator while recording. Recording behavior when you switch apps can vary by platform:
- Apple: Recording continues in the background and is shown by a flashing orange Mic icon in the status bar. Tap this icon to return to Goodnotes.
- Windows and Web: Recording continues in the background.
- Android: Recording stops when you switch to a different app (it doesn’t continue in the background).
1. When you’re done, tap the Stop Recording icon in the floating control to finish the recording. A new audio clip is created.

#### Play back a recording
1. Choose the Overflow menu > Show Recordings.
> 
For a ‣, you need to open the Sidebar (top-left corner), then the Audio tab.
1. Choose an audio clip from the Sidebar and tap the Play icon in the playback control at the bottom:
- 1.0x: Sets the playback speed
- Rewind: Rewinds the playback 10 seconds
- Play: Plays/Resumes the playback. Any notes previously taken during the recording will be played back as well. Once tapped, it’s changed to a Pause ⏸️ icon that can be used to pause the playback. Drag the Seek Bar to jump to a specific part of the clip.
- Fast-forward: Fast forward the playback 10 seconds.
- More (…): Shows Audio settings and other info.
- Noise Reduction: Reduces the recorded background noise during playback.
> 
This option is only available on devices using the A16 / M1 chip or later.
Find out what chip your device is using:
- iPad / iPhone: 
1. Find the Model Name line in device Settings > General > About.
1. Check System-on-chip for the Model Name: iPad | iPhone.
- Mac: Find the Chip line from Apple menu  in the left corner of your screen > About This Mac.
- Notes Playback: Controls how your linked notes appear during playback.
- Spotlight (default): Notes ahead of the playback position appear faded, while notes that have already played remain fully visible.
- Real-time reveal: Your handwriting is revealed progressively during playback, as if it’s being written in real time.
- Static: All handwriting stays fully visible during playback (no fading or reveal animation).
> 
Limitations:
- Platforms: available on Apple platforms only. These settings do not sync across platforms at this time.
- Document types: available for notebook documents only (not available for Whiteboards or Text Documents).

#### Play back some notes linked to the recording
1. Select those notes, or long-press any part of them.
1. Choose Play Recording. The playback jumps to the audio clip where those notes were taken.
> Notes are only captured on the recording's starting page. If you start recording in one document and switch to another, those notes won't sync with playback.

### Audio Transcription & Summary

#### Audio Transcription
Goodnotes offers two transcription methods:
- On-device Transcription works right on your device without sending audio to the internet. This gives better privacy but uses more battery. You need a newer device, must download language packages, and have fewer language options.
- Cloud Transcription sends your audio to secure servers for processing. This uses less battery and works on all compatible devices with all supported languages. No downloads are needed, though your audio does travel to the cloud with privacy protections in place.
For devices and subscription plans that support both methods, Cloud Transcription automatically activates when you're online, while On-device Transcription takes over when you're offline.
| Cloud Transcription | Feature | On-device Transcription |
| Audio processed in secure cloud | Privacy | Audio stays on device |
| Lower consumption | Battery usage | Higher consumption |
| Requires active internet connection | Internet requirement | Only for initial model download |
| 99+ languages | Language support | English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Thai |
| No download required | Setup | Requires downloading language model |
| Continuous usage throughout session | AI Credit usage | None |
| Works on all compatible devices | Device requirements | - iPad / iPhone: A15 chip or M1 and newer, iOS / iPadOS 17 or later. 
- Mac: Apple Silicon M1 or newer, macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later. |
| Pro, AI Pass, CN-Essential, CN-Special Edition | Subscription requirements | Free: 20-min recording cap, 3-document cap
Essential: No caps. Essential in China is treated as Pro |

#### Audio Summary
Basic Summarization provides a complete overview of your recording after it finishes, highlighting main points and decisions. Live Summarization operates in real-time during recording, continuously updating as your meeting progresses to help you track important information immediately.
| Basic Summarization | Feature | Live Summarization |
| Manual after recording completes | Generation | Real-time during recording |
| Entire recording analyzed at once | Content coverage | Updates as meeting progresses |
| One-time usage per summary | AI Credit usage | Continuous usage throughout session |
| All compatible devices | Device Requirements | All compatible devices |
| Free, Essential, Special Edition | Subscription Requirements | Pro, AI Pass, CN-Essential, CN-Special Edition |

#### How Audio Transcription and Summary work together
1. With either transcription method active, start a recording and watch as a live transcript with timestamped entries gradually appears in the Transcript tab.
1. When you open the Summary tab, you'll either see a Get Basic Summary button (only after the recording has stopped) or a live summary of the conversation in progress.
> 
You can drag a line from the Summary or Transcript tab on to the page to add to your notes.

## Whiteboard Document


#### Overview
A Whiteboard Document is a new document format featuring an infinite canvas, giving users greater freedom for note-taking, planning, collaborative brainstorming, mind-mapping and diagramming.

#### Create a Whiteboard
1. From the Documents tab of the Library View, choose +New… > Whiteboard.
1. Set properties of the Whiteboard: Name, Language, Pattern, Color.
1. (Optional) Select a built-in template to get started quickly.
1. Choose Create.

#### Manage boards
A Whiteboard document can contain multiple boards, similar to how a regular notebook contains pages.
To manage boards in the current Whiteboard document:
1. Open the Sidebar (leftmost icon on the toolbar).
1. Do any of the following:
- Add a new board: Choose Add Board.
- Switch to a board: Choose it.
- Rearrange boards: Long-press one and drag it to a new position.
- Edit a board or take an action on it: Open the board’s menu and choose an action:
- Rename: Change the name of the board.
- Duplicate: Create a duplicate of the board in the current document.
- Move to… or Move: Move the board to another Whiteboard document.
- (Apple only) Export: Export the board.
- (Apple only) Open in New Window: Open the board in Split View.
- Move to Trash: Delete the board.
> 
(Apple only) Swipe across a board and choose the Trash icon to delete it.
- Take an action on multiple boards:
1. Select the boards:
iOS/iPadOS
1. Open More (…) menu (top-left corner) > Select Boards.
1. Choose the board(s), or choose Select All:
Android, Windows, and Web
1. Enter Selection Mode in the Sidebar (top-left corner icon).
1. Select the radio button on each board thumbnail, or choose Select All.
1. Choose an action at the top, or from the More menu:
- (Apple only) Export: Export the selected board(s) (Apple only).
- (Apple only) Mark as Seen: Acknowledge the changes others make on the board(s). See ‣.
- Move: Move the selected board(s) to another Whiteboard document.
- Move to Trash: Delete the selected board(s).

#### Change the properties of a board
1. Open the board from the Sidebar.
1. Open the More (…) menu.
1. Change the properties: Background Pattern and/or Background Color.

#### (Apple only) Navigate your Whiteboard quickly

Ways to use the minimap:
- Drag within the minimap to quickly pan across different areas.
- Double-tap the minimap to fit the content to screen.
- Use the zoom button to adjust your view level.
- Choose the bottom-right Minimap button (next to the zoom button) to toggle it.

When you change a notebook to a Whiteboard, you get a much bigger space to work with. This lets you easily place and link your ideas.

#### (Apple only) Know the differences between editable and flattened PDF Formats
When you export a page or document to a PDF, you can choose between the Editable and Flattened PDF formats. Flattened PDFs (the only format Goodnotes for Android, Windows, and Web currently supports) should work for you most of the times as they are lighter in file size, compatible with most PDF viewers, and any handwriting in them is searchable just like regular text. However, if you want to retain the outlines or hyperlinks in the exported PDF, choosing the Editable PDF Format is required. We summarize the differences below.
Editable
- Handwriting is not searchable.
- Objects (images, text boxes, handwriting, highlighting) on the page can be selected and moved/resized in other PDF viewers.
- Document outlines are preserved.
- Hyperlinks in the original PDF text are preserved.
Flattened
- Handwriting can be searched, selected, and copied if "Enable Handwriting Recognition" is enabled in the Export dialog. Copying the handwriting will copy the recognized characters to the clipboard.
- Document outlines are not preserved.
- Hyperlinks in the original PDF text are not preserved.
- Recommended for better viewing results in a PDF Viewer

### Goodnotes Cloud Q&A


#### What is Goodnotes Cloud?
Goodnotes Cloud is a cloud storage feature that allows you to back up and sync your notes, documents, and other content created in the Goodnotes app. By enabling Goodnotes Cloud, you can securely store your files online and access them across multiple devices. Goodnotes Cloud is being rolled out. Goodnotes Cloud powers Goodnotes on Android, Windows, and Web.

#### Is my personal data safe in Goodnotes Cloud?
Yes, your data is securely stored and encrypted. Goodnotes uses third-party cloud providers (such as Amazon Web Services) to power the infrastructure for Goodnotes Cloud. These providers are required to protect your data under strict data processing and confidentiality agreements.

#### Does Goodnotes access or collect data from my notes or documents?
No, Goodnotes does not access or collect any data from your notes or documents stored in Goodnotes Cloud unless you give explicit permission. We only store the files to provide backup and syncing services.

#### Who can access my notes and documents stored in Goodnotes Cloud?
Only you can access the notes and documents stored in your Goodnotes Cloud account, unless you choose to share them with others. You can share files with specific individuals through collaboration features in the Goodnotes app.

#### How long does Goodnotes keep my data in the cloud?
Your data is retained in Goodnotes Cloud for as long as you maintain your Goodnotes account and continue using the service. If you delete your account or delete specific documents, the data will be removed from Goodnotes Cloud.

#### What happens to my Goodnotes Cloud data if my account becomes inactive?
Your data in Goodnotes Cloud will remain available as long as your account is active and you continue using the service. However, as outlined in clause 12.2 of our Terms & Conditions, we may delete your account or any data stored in Goodnotes Cloud if your account remains inactive for 2 years without payment. Before any data is removed, we will send reminder emails, giving you the chance to either download your notes as PDFs or Goodnotes files, or renew your subscription to continue accessing them.
It’s important to note that this policy applies only to Goodnotes Cloud. Whether you're a subscription or one-time payment user, any notes stored locally or in iCloud are yours forever, and we will never alter or delete them. You will receive multiple warnings before any Cloud data is removed. For further details, please refer to Section 12.2 of our Terms.

#### Can I transfer my data out of Goodnotes Cloud?
Yes, simply open Goodnotes menu > Cloud & Backup > Cloud Sync > Manual Backup Documents > Back Up Now.

#### Where is my data stored?
Goodnotes Cloud uses third-party providers like Amazon Web Services to store your data. Your data may be transferred to servers located outside your country, including outside the UK or EEA. Goodnotes ensures that all data transfers comply with legal requirements to protect your personal data.

#### What happens if I disable Goodnotes Cloud?
If you disable Goodnotes Cloud, your notes and documents will no longer be synced to Goodnotes Cloud. Files already sync in Goodnotes Cloud will remain until you delete them manually, but new files will not be synced if Goodnotes Cloud is disabled.

#### What happens if there is a data breach?
Goodnotes takes data security seriously and has measures in place to protect your personal data. In the event of a data breach, Goodnotes will notify you and any applicable regulators if required by law. We also have procedures to respond quickly to such incidents.
For more detailed information, please refer to the full Terms & Conditions and Privacy Policy available on our website.

You can link to specific pages within a Notebook or Whiteboard, but you cannot link to a specific section or location on a page or board.

No, links can only be added to typed text. However, you can convert your handwriting to text first, then add a link to the converted text.

No, you can only link to external websites and documents within your Goodnotes library.

No, phone numbers and email addresses are not automatically converted to clickable links.

### Folders & documents


## A folder versus a document
GoodNotes provides an organizational hierarchy you're likely already familiar with. It's very similar to that of folders and files on a computer.
A folder is like a virtual bookshelf in your GoodNotes library. Physical shelves are useful because they group books together (typically by subject) in order to help you quickly find a book of interest. For example, if you want to find a Calculus book, look for it on the "Science" or "Mathematics" shelf. Want to find a Christianity book? Start with the "World Religions" shelf. In GoodNotes, you would organize this the same way by creating folders named "Science" and "World Religions" and put in them relevant documents, like "Buddhism," "Christianity," "Geography," "Physics."
A document refers to either a notebook that you create in GoodNotes, or one imported from a file for annotating. A document must contain at least one page and can be organized in a folder.
While you can always use our Search feature to find a document, keeping your library organized with folders is recommended in case you want to find all similar documents.
Unlike a physical library, however, GoodNotes offers an extra benefit: You can create an unlimited number of folders and sub-folders (that is, folders within folders) to suit your needs.
Here are two example use cases. If you're a student taking notes for classes, you can create a folder named, say, "Spring 2020 Semester" for documents like "Calculus 101" and "Creative Writing 4." If you're working for different companies, create a folder for each to store respective documents like "Logo design" and "Project Requirements."

## Know the differences between editable and flattened PDF Formats
You can export a document in either format with these properties in the output PDF:

### Editable
- Handwriting is not searchable.
- Objects (images, text boxes, handwriting, highlighting) on the page can be selected and moved/resized in other PDF viewers.
- Document outlines are preserved.
- Hyperlinks in the original PDF text are preserved.

### Flattened
- Handwriting can be searched, selected, and copied if "Enable Handwriting Recognition" is enabled in the Export dialog. Copying the handwriting will copy the recognized characters to the clipboard.
- Document outlines are not preserved.
- Hyperlinks in the original PDF text are not preserved.
- Recommended for better viewing results in a PDF Viewer

### iCloud Sync


## What It Does
iCloud sync allows storing GoodNotes documents in iCloud and keeping them up to date (that is, synchronized or synced) between your iPhones, iPads and Macs. For example, you can start editing a document on one device and resume working on the document on another device.
Even if you have only one device, keeping your documents in iCloud will help in case you need to reinstall the app or reset the device. After a fresh GoodNotes reinstallation, you can simply start the app, enable iCloud sync and have all your documents redownloaded from iCloud.

## How to Use It

#### Set up iCloud sync
Since iCloud sync involves data transfer between your devices, please do the following on all of them:
1. Sign into the same Apple ID.
1. On your iOS / iPadOS devices, open the Settings app, go to Apple ID > iCloud, and make sure both "iCloud Drive" and "GoodNotes" are enabled.
1. On your Mac, open System Preferences, go to iCloud, enable "iCloud Drive". Click on Options... next to "iCloud Drive" and also enable "GoodNotes" in the list.
1. Open GoodNotes menu.
- ▸ How?
1. Choose Settings > iCloud Settings and enable Use iCloud.

## Troubleshooting
After you turn on "Use iCloud" in GoodNotes iCloud Settings, your GoodNotes documents should be automatically and seamlessly backed up to Apple's iCloud servers. In some cases, however, you might notice your documents aren't syncing properly as they should. Symptoms include error messages about iCloud, inaccessible greyed-out documents, cloud icons with "!" on specific documents.

### Things to Try First
- Check your Internet connection. Believe it or not, we've seen a few iCloud sync issues on devices where Wi-Fi was disabled, sluggish, or unstable!
- Check your iCloud storage. Try freeing up your iCloud storage anyway and see if the issue is resolved.
- Check iCloud server reliability. Occasional iCloud service failures are more common than you think.
- Force-quit the app (on iOS/iPadOS or macOS) and relaunch it.
- Update your GoodNotes version. (The App Store / Mac App Store might not be aware there's a newer GoodNotes version.)
- Update your iOS/iPadOS version, and/or macOS version.
- Disable Use iCloud in GoodNotes iCloud Settings, tap Done, and enable it again.
- Restart your devices (iOS/iPadOS or macOS) to restart the local iCloud service on each that communicates with iCloud servers.
- Check that you've set up iCloud sync on all your devices correctly.
- Leave GoodNotes running in the foreground (i.e. visible on the screen) for it to work more efficiently. After typing text, exit text input mode by clicking/tapping outside the text box. If you're working on huge PDF documents (like textbooks), expect several minutes before the changes appear on your other devices.
- Log out of your iCloud account and log back in.
- Turn off iCloud Private Relay (iPadOS 15 only).
- Disable content-blocking or network-security apps, like AdGuard, Eero Secure.

### Automatic backup


## What It Does
This feature automatically uploads your changed documents to Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive for safekeeping while you're working in GoodNotes. It's best used with iCloud sync to protect your notes so that you can always find them in the auto-backup folder in case something destructive occurs to your local library.

## How It Works
Once initially set up, auto-backup will upload all your documents to the auto-backup folder. Afterward, only changed documents get backed up. 
> Auto-backup is a one-way process; it doesn't sync your changes across your devices as iCloud does. Changes you make in the cloud storage won't affect your GoodNotes library.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between iCloud Sync and Automatic Backup?
The main difference is that Auto Backup is a one-way process that uploads a copy of your documents to the auto-backup cloud storage, whereas ‣ is a two-way process that synchronizes one version of your documents across different devices. We list more differences below:
| Auto Backup | iCloud Sync |
| No | Yes | Syncing across devices |
| Yes. You can find these files on the cloud storage.  | No. The documents are encrypted on iCloud servers. | Accessing backed-up files |
| Manual | Automatic
 | Re-importing documents from the cloud |

### How should I back up my documents?
Using iCloud Sync is sufficient for security reasons, for example in case you lose or damage your device. Your data is still saved in iCloud and will download to GoodNotes on other devices that use the same iCloud account. We recommend setting up auto-backup when you can't or don't want to use iCloud, or when you need a way to access your documents online on a computer where GoodNotes is not installed.

### How are my data and privacy protected when I grant GoodNotes access to my cloud storage account?
The app itself needs explicit "Read & Write Access" to your cloud storage in order to save and modify files on the cloud storage. Of course, we won't be able to see or modify any of your documents on the cloud storage.

### Why is iCloud Drive not an option?
iCloud Drive doesn't provide the necessary integrations that would allow connecting the Auto Backup feature. Since GoodNotes is available for macOS and iOS/iPadOS, you can enable iCloud Sync instead in order to synchronize your notes across devices to access them through the GoodNotes app directly.

#### Why Full-page Typing?
Though GoodNotes is widely known the world over as a handwriting app, we also want to provide a good typing experience for those who prefer to type instead of writing notes.

#### What are typing-optimized templates?
These templates are specially designed to ensure proper line spacing between lines of text. If such a template contains visible lines, typed text will sit neatly on them. Look for those templates marked with a green keyboard icon:

#### Can I type properly spaced lines of text on my own PDF template?
Unfortunately, that's not guaranteed as the line spacing will be set to default and any lines on the template are ignored. For this reason, you're recommended to use one of our typing-optimized templates.

Absolutely. Changes to those pages will be visible to every participant. See also: Share a document for collaboration

## What It Does
The Laser Pointer is used to focus attention on some parts of your notes while you're presenting them. It's useful for a variety of purposes. For example:
- ▸ Teaching
- ▸ Recording helpful videos
- ▸ Creating quick presentations and transitions

## How to Use It

### Step 1: Share your GoodNotes screen
This step is actually optional but the Laser Pointer is most useful when you're sharing your GoodNotes screen. There are two ways you can share your screen.

#### Screen Sharing (or Screen Broadcasting)
This kind of screen sharing lets you "broadcast" everything on your screen, including notifications, to another app. For example, you might be in a video conferencing app on your device and want to share your screen to the other attendees. To do that, follow these steps:
1. Join a meeting with your video conferencing app, e.g. Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams.
1. Locate and tap a button/icon in that app that says something like Share Content or Share Screen.
1. Tap Start Broadcast.
1. Switch to GoodNotes to have its screen shared.

#### Screen Mirroring via AirPlay
Screen Mirroring allows projecting your device screen (that is, mirroring it) onto an external screen that supports AirPlay, which is Apple's wireless communication protocol. Typical AirPlay receivers include Apple TV, Macs and some Smart TVs. By sharing your screen via this protocol, GoodNotes enters what's called Presentation Mode, which gives you more control, like hiding part of the screen you don't want to share with the audience.
For screen mirroring instructions, see ‣.

### Step 2: Use the Laser Pointer

#### Focus attention on some notes
- Select the Laser Pointer from the toolbar, then draw on the screen to add temporary annotations, or circle noteworthy information.
> If you don't see the tool, swipe left on the toolbar, or turn your iPad to landscape.

#### Change the pointer style
- Choose either of these from the contextual section:
- Dot: A red dot will follow along your touch on the screen, much like one created by a physical laser pointer remote.
- Trail: Drawing on the screen will add a temporary laser "trail," which will disappear after a second unless you keep touching the screen.

### The Zoom Window Tool


## What It Does
The Zoom Window Tool lets you write neatly, especially with your large finger or capacitive stylus. By using this tool, you only need to focus on a Zoom Window, which shows a magnified view of an area specified by a blue box. With auto-advance enabled by default, you can write continuously in the fixed Zoom Window view without worrying about scrolling the page.

## How to Use It

#### Write with the Zoom Window Tool
1. Select the Zoom Window tool from the toolbar.
1. Write from left to right in the Zoom Window that appears at the bottom. As soon as you cross the middle of the Zoom Window with any pen stroke, a blue area will appear both on the left side of the window and on the page. Continue writing in this area in order to automatically move the blue box on the page forward. Auto-advance can be disabled as instructed below.

#### Exit Zoom Window
Do any of the following:
- Tap the Zoom Window Tool from the toolbar again.
- Select a tool other than the Pen, Erase, Highlighter, and Shape tools. 
- Tap the Exit Zoom Window icon in the top-right corner of the Zoom Window.
- ▸ Show the Zoom Window Controls

#### Reposition the Zoom Box
- Tap on the new location on the page.
- Drag the box to the new location.
- Tap one of the navigation icons near the top-right corner of the Zoom Window:
- ▸ Show the Zoom Window Controls
- Back: Move the box back.
- Forward: Move the box forward.
- New Line: Move the box to the next line at the left margin.

#### Scale the Zoom Box
You can scale the Zoom Box to cover a smaller or larger focus area. The smaller the box, the higher the magnification level of the Zoom Window. 
1. Find the Diagonal Resize handle in the bottom-right corner of the Zoom Box.
- ▸ Show the Zoom Window Controls
1. Drag the handle diagonally.

#### Change the height of the Zoom Box
1. Find the Vertical Resize handle in the bottom-right corner of the Zoom Window.
- ▸ Show the Zoom Window Controls
1. Drag the handle up/down.
> If you don't see the Vertical Resize handle, rotate your device to portrait, grab the top border of the Zoom Window and drag it up all the way to reveal the handle.

## How to Customize It

#### Increase the zoom level
- Scale the Zoom Box towards its center to make it smaller.
- Spread with 2 fingers in the Zoom Window.

#### Decrease the zoom level
- Scale the Zoom Box away from its center to make it larger.
- Pinch with 2 fingers in the Zoom Window.

#### Reposition the Zoom Window
1. Find the Move Zoom View handle at the middle-top of the Zoom Window.
- ▸ Show the Zoom Window Controls
1. Drag the handle up/down.

#### Enable / Disable auto-advance
1. Tap the More icon in the top-right corner.
1. Choose Document Editing.
1. Toggle Auto Advance.

#### Adjust the left margin
You can adjust the left margin so that when the Zoom Box moves to the next line, it will start at the specified indent. This is useful if you want to write within a right-hand column, for example.
- Tap the Increase / Decrease Left Margin icon in the top-left corner of the Zoom Window
- ▸ Show the Zoom Window Controls

#### Adjust the return height
With auto-advance enabled, the Zoom Window relies on a default return height in order to know how far down to move to the next line. With auto-advance disabled, tapping the New Line icon will also move this far to the next line.
To change this value for a specific template:
1. Choose Notebook Templates.
1. Tap Edit in the upper right and then on the template name (where you see three dots).
1. Enter a desired value (in points) in the Height field and tap Save.
> Note that this change will only take effect for new notebooks using that template. If you want the new change to apply to the existing template, change the template to another and then change back.

GoodNotes comes with a wide variety of covers/templates that should meet the most common note-taking needs. If your work requires special or personalized templates, however, you can add them all to GoodNotes.
Every PDF or image file can be used as a custom cover or paper template.

## Add custom templates
1. Choose Notebook Templates.
1. Select the paper size and layout that match those of the covers/templates to be added. For example, if you’re adding an A4-sized template in portrait (i.e. vertical orientation), select “A4” from the first dropdown list and “Portrait” from the second:

- Add a custom cover: Tap the Default Cover preview in the upper section.
- Add a custom paper template: Tap the Default Paper preview.

> You can create your own group by tapping +  next to Edit in the top-right corner.
1. Choose an import method:
- Add one or more covers/templates from the Files app (recommended): Tap From Files.
> For best results, add a PDF instead of an image. For example, add an A4-sized PDF instead of an image having the same dimensions in pixels of the A4 size.
> If you add a multi-page PDF as a custom cover/template, only its first page is added. If you want to use the PDF as a template document (e.g. a multi-page contract), import it to your library instead and duplicate it.

### Presentation Mode

GoodNotes Presentation Mode allows you to convert your iPad or iPhone into a digital whiteboard (currently not supported in GoodNotes for macOS). When you connect your device to an external screen or projector via HDMI or AirPlay Screen Mirroring, Presentation Mode lets you hide the user interface and other distracting elements from your audience.

## Trigger Presentation Mode
1. Connect your iPad / iPhone to the same Wi-Fi network as your Apple TV, AirPlay 2-compatible smart TV, or Mac.
1. Swipe down from the upper-right corner of the screen.
1. Tap the Screen Mirroring icon (next to the Rotation Lock icon).
1. Select the external screen, e.g. Apple TV, AirPlay 2-compatible smart TV, or Mac from the list.
1. If prompted, enter the on-screen passcode on your iPad / iPhone.
> To stop screen mirroring, repeat steps 2-3 and choose Stop Mirroring.

## Choose how your notes are presented
With Presentation Mode active, do the following:
1. Select one of these options:
| What is shown on the external screen | Option |
| Exactly what you see on your device. | Mirror Entire Screen |
| The current page with zooming and page switching animations. Apps in Split View are hidden. | Mirror Presenter Page |
| The current page in full width and height without zooming and page switching animations. Apps in Split View are hidden. | Mirror Full Page |
> Presentation Mode over AirPlay (Screen Mirroring) requires an AirPlay receiver, which means if you want to mirror your iPad or iPhone screen to your typical computer screen, you'll need to turn the computer into an AirPlay receiver (unless the functionality is already built in). If you're on macOS Monterey or later, the Mac is already an AirPlay receiver. Otherwise, you will need to use a third-party app, like Zoom or Reflector, in order to make your computer an AirPlay receiver, which makes it possible to use Presentation Mode in GoodNotes.f

### Comments

Adding a comment is a space-saving way to share thoughts on various sections of your notes with other collaborators on a shared document, or simply with yourself!
Example use cases include:
- Education: Teachers and students can comment on a math problem on a shared document.
- Self-study: Students who have a notebook full of notes might add comments to specific areas on the page, forming “collapsible” sticky notes.
- Collaboration: Interior designers can share their furniture selections on a shared floor plan, allowing clients to share thoughts on the furniture suggested on the page.

## Add a comment thread

1. Type a comment, and choose the Send (airplane) button. Further comments to a thread can be added this way.
> Non-users of GoodNotes can also add comments to a document made available on the web.
1. While viewing the document in a desktop browser, right-click on a specific location on the page where you want to add a comment thread, and choose Add Comment. If you’re not already signed in to an Apple or Google account, continue to click Sign in. (Anonymous comments are not currently supported.)

## Open a comment thread

### Flashcards (experimental)

GoodNotes lets you create and study flashcards using Spaced Repetition. Put simply, by employing the technique to space out your learning, you can retain more information with less time. And to that end, all you need to do is do a bit of setup and let GoodNotes handle the rest.
> This is a legacy feature. See ‣.

## Flashcard Quick Start
1. Choose GoodNotes Standard or A7 (iPhone) from the first dropdown list (Paper Size), choose Portrait from the second dropdown list (Orientation) and scroll the Essentials category all the way to the right to see the Flashcard template (the very last one in this category):

1. On a new page, write the question in the top half of the page, and the answer in the bottom half. These halves respectively correspond to the Front & Back sides of a physical flashcard.
1. Repeat the previous step for any other question-answer pair to form a flashcard deck.
1. Open the More menu (...) in the top-right corner > Study Flashcards.
1. For each flashcard, you will see the question (Front side) first. Tap the screen to show the corresponding answer (Back side).
1. Once the answer is shown, indicate the difficulty of the question based on how easily you were able to recall the answer:
- Again: I did not remember the answer. I need to try again soon.
- Hard: I remembered the answer, but only with great difficulty.
- Good: I remembered the answer, but had to think a little.
- Easy: I remembered the answer easily.
The next time you activate Study Flashcards mode, GoodNotes will determine the best order in which each card is shown again in order to aid your recall. Continue to indicate the difficulties of the questions to help GoodNotes perfect the review order for you.

### Reset your learning progress
- From the Flashcards screen, tap the icon in the top-right corner > Reset learning progress, and confirm.
> Study Flashcards mode starts only when there is at least one flashcard (i.e. a page in a flashcard template) in the current notebook, and non-flashcard pages are skipped in this mode.

### Enable flashcards functionality for a paper template
The built-in flashcard template isn’t yet available in all paper sizes or orientations, but you can mark any template as a flashcard template:
1. Enable Flashcard Template option and tap Save.
When a page in a flashcard template is created, its upper half is reserved for the "Question" section and the lower half for "Answer".

### Learn with Study Sets

GoodNotes not only helps you capture good notes; it can actually assist you with learning from them through the use of study sets!
A study set is essentially a specialized GoodNotes document consisting of one or more flashcards, which, like their physical versions, are cards containing words, images, or other information on one or both sides, used for learning or memorizing information.
The app offers both Practice Mode and Smart Learning Mode for different learning needs. Practice Mode allows you to review all flashcards in one go, either in sequential or random order, which is useful for last-minute cramming sessions. Smart Learn Mode uses a scientifically proven technique called spaced repetition to predict when you're likely to forget a card and bring it up in your next session.
> Study sets are available across all of your devices via ‣, so you can conveniently review them no matter where you are.

## Create a study set

### From scratch
1. Choose Study Set.
1. Title it and tap Create.
1. Edit the Question and Answer sides of the first card:
1. Choose a content type: Text, Image, or Freeform
1. Tap the content area and insert new content.
1. Do one or more of the following:
- Insert a new card: Tap the “Add card” icon near the top-right corner.
- Duplicate the current card: Open the More menu (...) in the top-right corner > Duplicate Card.
- Delete the current card: Open the More menu (...) > Delete Card.
- Navigate through cards:
- Go to the previous/next card: Swipe left/right (iOS/iPadOS), or use the left/right keys on the (hardware) keyboard (iPadOS/macOS). See also ‣.
- Jump to a card: Choose the thumbnail view icon and select the card. 
1. Repeat steps 4 and 5 until you have a complete set.
> Save time by dragging and dropping content from another GoodNotes document in Split View to your study set in Edit mode.

### From a flashcard notebook
Since Study Sets is a vastly improved version of the ‣ feature, you’re recommended to make the switch for a more efficient learning experience. Until a mass migration tool is available, you can convert a flashcard notebook to a study set:
1. Open the notebook.
1. Open the More menu (...) in the top-right corner > Convert Flashcards to Study Set. Once successful, the new study set will be automatically opened.
1. Optionally delete the original notebook to save space.

### From an external file
You can import these types of files into GoodNotes as study sets:
- study sets exported from the app, e.g. by a study partner.
- plain text files in .txt, .csv, or .tsv format created by a spreadsheet app or another flashcard app. See ‣.

#### Migrate from another flashcard app
If a third-party app meets the following criteria, you can migrate your flashcards in it to GoodNotes:
- The flashcards in that third-party app contain pure textual content, i.e. no rich content like videos or images.
- The third-party app can export its flashcards to a plain text file in a GoodNotes-readable structure. See ‣.
For example, here’s how to create an importable file from two popular flashcard apps.
For Quizlet
1. Open a set in that app.
1. Open the "..." menu > Export.
1. Set "Between term and definition" to Tab, and “New line” for "Between rows".
1. Select Copy Text.
1. Open a spreadsheet app, e.g. Google Sheets, right-click the top left cell in an empty spreadsheet > Paste Special > Paste values only.
1. Go to File > Download > Comma Separated Values (.csv) or Tab Separated Values (.tsv).
For Anki
1. Navigate to the deck you want to export from the start screen.
1. Press the "⚙️ (Settings)" button next to the deck > Export.
1. Set the export format as "Notes in Plain Text".
Once you have the exported file as above, simply import it to GoodNotes to create a new study set.

## Review a study set
1. Open the set.
1. Choose a review mode:
- Learn the set sequentially or randomly: Tap Study > Practice.
- Flip the card: Tap it.
- Go to the previous/next card: Tap ← Previous or Next →
- Go to a card: Drag the slider left/right and release where the card is.
- Speak the text in view: Tap the Speaker icon, usable for textual content only.
- Switch between sequential and random order: Toggle the shuffle (middle) icon.
- Show Question or Answer first: Toggle the flip icon next to the shuffle icon.

- ❌ Still Learning: I did not remember the information. I need to try again soon.
